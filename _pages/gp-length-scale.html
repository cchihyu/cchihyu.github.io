--- layout: single title: "Choose the Length Scale: Gaussian Process Regression" permalink: /gp-length-scale/ author_profile: true ---

A Gaussian process (GP) with mean 0 and squared exponential kernel

\[ k(x, x') = \exp\!\left( -\frac{(x - x')^2}{2\ell^2} \right) \]

is used to learn an unknown function \(f\) on \([0, 1]\) from a few observations \(y_i = f(x_i) + \varepsilon_i\), where the noise \(\varepsilon_i \sim N(0, \sigma_n^2)\) has a known standard deviation \(\sigma_n\). The length scale \(\ell\) controls how quickly the GP believes \(f\) can change. Given the training points, the GP gives a posterior mean (its best guess of \(f\)) and a 95% credible band around it.

Your task: the true \(f\) is hidden. Looking only at the training points, choose a length scale so that the 95% band covers \(f\) everywhere on \([0, 1]\), while keeping the band as narrow as possible. Then click Reveal f to see how you did. A tiny \(\ell\) covers almost anything, but its band is too wide to be useful. With noise, the training points no longer sit exactly on \(f\), so the GP should not pass through them; try a few noise draws to see how much the best \(\ell\) changes.
