A Gaussian process (GP) with mean 0 and squared exponential kernel

\[ k(x, x') = \exp\!\left( -\frac{(x - x')^2}{2\ell^2} \right) \]

is used to learn an unknown function \(f\) on \([0, 1]\) from a few observations. The length scale \(\ell\) controls how quickly the GP believes \(f\) can change. Given the observations, the GP gives a posterior mean (its best guess of \(f\)) and a 95% credible band around it.

How to play: the true \(f\) is hidden. You start with 2 observations. In each round, move the slider to choose a length scale, then click Submit. You will be told what percentage of \(f\) on \([0, 1]\) your 95% band covers, and you will get one more observation. The game ends when you have 10 observations, and then \(f\) is revealed. Aim for full coverage with a band as narrow as possible: a tiny \(\ell\) covers almost anything, but its band is too wide to be useful.
