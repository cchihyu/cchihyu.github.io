How to play

Imagine you are a baker trying to create the tastiest cake. Every cake you bake gets a taste score out of 5, but you do not know which recipe gives the best taste.

  • 1-D games: you control the amount of sugar (0–200 g), and you can bake 10 cakes.
  • 2-D games: you control the amount of sugar and the oven temperature (150–230 °C), and you can bake 20 cakes.
  1. Choose a game from the menu below. Everyone who picks the same game bakes with the same hidden taste function, so scores can be compared.
  2. Click on the plot to choose a recipe and bake a cake. Its taste is revealed straight away. Bake a cake scoring above 4.5 and you have made a masterpiece; score below 1 and it comes out burnt.
  3. Use what you have tasted so far to decide on the next recipe, until you have used up all your cakes.
  4. When the game is over, the true taste of every recipe is revealed, together with your score.

Your goal is to find the recipe with the best taste. But every cake you bake is sold to a customer, so every bad cake costs you. Your score is the cumulative regret: for each cake, how much taste it lost compared with the best possible cake x*, added up over all T cakes,

RT = ∑t=1T ( f(x*) − f(xt) ),

where f(xt) is the taste of your t-th cake. Lower is better. To do well you have to balance exploring new recipes against exploiting the best recipe you have found so far.
